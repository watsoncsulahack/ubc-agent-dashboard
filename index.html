Agent Economy. The Market Brief.

A structured research snapshot for builders and judges: hackathon rules & cash prizes, observed on-chain machine payments, Solana vs. Circle Arc ecosystem metrics, consumer trust barriers, and sourced failure records.

SNAPSHOT · NOV 16–17, 2026
Newcomer Guide · 30-Sec Gist AI agents are beginning to pay for APIs and goods—but human trust and spending guardrails lag behind.
1. What is the "Agent Economy"? Software agents can now hold wallets or scoped payment tokens and pay per API call (using protocols like x402 and MPP). Today, 80.8% of labeled on-chain agent payment volume buys AI model inference—not retail shopping.
2. Why is consumer adoption only 11%? While 300M+ people use AI shopping assistants for research, 70% want to review/override purchases before payment, and 76% feel uncomfortable with autonomous spending. Winning products enforce hard spending caps and human approvals.
3. How to read this dashboard Every metric carries its exact timeframe and source. Never add different denominators (e.g., 30-day x402 transfers vs. cumulative volume vs. 2030 McKinsey forecasts, or raw identity mints vs. active agents).

Observed Evidence

What is demonstrably working today?

  • AI-assisted product discovery: 300M+ consumers use tools like Amazon Rufus for search and comparisons.
  • Pay-per-query machine APIs: Observable on-chain micropayments (median $0.010) settle inference and data queries via x402 and MPP.
  • Scoped payment credentials: Stripe Link/SPT, Visa TAP, and Mastercard Agent Pay ship tokenization and per-purchase confirmation controls.
Evidence Gaps

What remains unproven or exaggerated?

  • True autonomous agent headcount: Wallet addresses and ERC-8004 identity mints are heavily inflated by scripts and batch minting.
  • Autonomous travel/retail checkout: Using AI to plan trips (27% KPMG) is not autonomous ticket booking (66% distrust booking, Expedia).
  • Industry-wide loss rates: Incident trackers document real failure modes, not a population-wide failure probability.

Probabilistic Risk Classifiers (e.g., Jev) vs. Hard Caps

TypeSafe AI's Jev constrains model output into typed shapes (Choice, Score, Noul) with token probabilities—useful for flagging ambiguous terms or risky intent.

What Typed Models Do Well Pre-screen unstructured merchant terms ("Is this nonrefundable?") and route low-confidence actions to human review.
What Typed Models Cannot Guarantee Schema validity is not semantic truth. A deterministic verifier (integer minor-unit caps, merchant allowlists, expiry timestamps) must gate fund release.
