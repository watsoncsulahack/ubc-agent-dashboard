The central distinction
Most reported does not mean most costly.

Phishing/spoofing leads the FBI's 2025 complaint counts. Investment fraud, business email compromise and tech-support scams lead in reported losses. Phishing can be an entry mechanism for other crimes, but category losses cannot be added together and attributed to phishing.

Product hypothesis

Explain the risk; don't simply assign a score.

Who claims to be contacting me? What do they want me to do? Where would that action send me? What evidence supports the conclusion?

Consumer application design

Trust matters more than the payment rail

The useful product is a phishing assistant that explains uncertainty and compensates useful verified evidence—not a wallet that treats every report as truth.

AI agentRisk interpretation and triage
Human participantContext and corrections
Policy engineVerification, eligibility, payout caps
Stablecoin settlementAuditable, bounded rewards

DeFi functions and prediction-market mechanics were omitted because they do not advance the core consumer-protection workflow.

Consumer

Safety at the point of click

Explain a suspicious message and its destination with separate confidence and evidence.

Agent

Evidence handling

Extract claimed identity, action, and contextual signals; send uncertain cases to human review.

Payments

Reward verified novelty

Release controlled testnet payments for useful contributions; avoid speculative token incentives.

Avoid

Pay every upload

Simple, but directly incentivizes duplication and fabricated submissions.

Candidate

Pay independently verified reports

Aligns rewards with evidence quality, but needs review capacity and an appeals mechanism.

Research direction

Pay for novel, verified context

Rewards previously unseen and actionable observations without claiming a novel URL is inherently malicious.

Token ownership does not prove honest behavior. Wallet creation is cheap, submissions can be fabricated, and a dishonest actor need not hold enough tokens to care about network value. Verification, reputation, anti-Sybil controls and bounded payout rules remain necessary.

Licensing candidate: AGPLv3 for network-facing core, with a separate policy for standards-based SDKs. This requires a formal licensing review before publication and does not compel third parties to share independently generated data.

Privacy guardrail: Avoid storing email contents, private identifiers or readily guessable unsalted hashes of private reports on a public blockchain. On-chain consistency does not establish that a reported incident was truthful.

Product and governance commitments

Release and reuse

  • Publish complete source with the chosen FOSS license.
  • Document how to self-host, fork and audit the code.
  • Publish machine-readable API and threat-intelligence schemas.
  • Keep trademark governance separate from software freedom.

Safety and stewardship

  • Use sandboxed analysis; never visit suspicious destinations in the user's session.
  • Publish false-positive and appeal policies.
  • Separate contributor earnings from any optional training game.
  • Do not confuse simulated testnet USDC with monetary compensation.
